# Ship the look Rigi already built

Rigi's biggest aesthetic gain is not a new technique. It is a default and art-direction decision. The codebase already contains most of what the 2023–2026 frontier calls "premium" terrain rendering: photo-fitted aerial perspective, Swiss multidirectional relief with sky-view factor and cast shadows, Alpine tints, Tanaka contours, distance-faded ink silhouettes, Oklab per-distance harmonisation, guided-filter edge refinement, PBR Neutral output with grain and dither, and PeakFinder/Berann-style panorama labels. **All of it is opt-in.** The default `classic` preset compiles none of the `LOOK_*` defines and is pixel-pinned to the original grey-haze, Lambert, five-stop-ramp, cyan-contour look (`src/lib/style/store.ts:19-22`, `src/lib/style/defaults.ts:1-4`, `src/lib/look/look-key.ts:1-31`). The landing hero and demo exports are rendered in that old look too (`scripts/demo/export-overlay.ts:21-36`). The recommendation is to keep `classic` as a frozen regression preset and ship a new curated default preset (called "signature" here). Before that flip, run a two-day still-and-preference pass, fix the haze-fit cache bug (CR-11), and re-capture the stale style baseline. After it come a handful of quick wins that make overlays read as part of the photo rather than drawn on glass: terrain shadow and haze applied to overlays, leader-line occlusion, grouped sub-summit labels, range-ordered reveals, and porting the /roll PCF drape into the single-photo drape. The bigger bets are a Berann-style palette material and a photo-to-3D projective camera tween for the world view and landing page, and opt-in "art modes" (relighting, sky replacement, NPR) that alter photo pixels. Those art modes need hard guardrails because the photo is evidence of the match. Several frontier tools are research-only (Apple SHARP, Careaga/Aksoy intrinsics, FLUX.1-dev ControlNets) and must not ship.

## The best look in the codebase is switched off by default

The audit's central finding changes how to read the rest of the frontier. The look system is mature in code. `ViewStyle` is data (`src/lib/style/types.ts:40-253`), resolved as `merge(CLASSIC, PRESETS[preset], overrides)` (`src/lib/style/presets.ts:352-357`). There are ten presets, and five of them (`photo-matched`, `swiss`, `berann`, `topo-ink`, `slope`) turn on the advanced shaders (`src/lib/style/presets.ts:336-342`). The earlier render note's top-10 list is largely built. Photo-fitted haze (`src/lib/look/haze-fit.ts`), Rayleigh/Mie atmosphere with Cornette-Shanks phase (`src/lib/look/atmosphere.ts`), NOAA/Meeus sun from EXIF (`src/lib/look/sun.ts`), a 12 km cast-shadow and SVF field (`src/lib/look/relief/field.ts`), guided-filter refinement (`src/lib/look/guided-filter.ts`), Oklab band harmonisation (`src/lib/look/color-stats.ts`) and screen-space ink (`src/lib/look/glsl/composite.ts`) all exist. Yet first-time users, the landing shots and the demo export see `classic`. The audit lists that look as grey exponential haze, a fixed sun at `(-0.5,-0.4,0.75)`, cyan contours with a dark casing, and an orange skyline. The labels are Manrope with a drop shadow (`src/lib/materials.ts:352-363`, `src/lib/style/defaults.ts:23-56, 132-161`). **The only visual flourish on by default is the bloom reveal** (`src/lib/reveal/config.ts:154-160`).

The cause is structural, not an oversight. The roadmap makes it a principle that "Classic view stays pixel-identical" (`reports/roadmap.md:16`), and the CI style-baseline enforces it. That baseline is currently stale at 0/16 identical (`reports/status.md:40`). Those two choices protect the renderer parity work, which was the right call while deck replaced three as default. They also lock the product into its least-developed look. The fix is to move the pixel-identity guarantee from "the default" to "the preset named classic". The regression gate keeps its meaning, and art direction is free to move.

### What the new default should contain

Not every built feature belongs in a default. `photo-matched` is the most "bespoke" preset, but it has three costs that argue against shipping it wholesale. It loads the roughly 4.5 MB photo sky-segmentation model (`src/lib/look/look-key.ts:51-56`). It runs a haze fit of about 50 ms at 1024² on each pose settle (`src/lib/look/haze-fit.ts:9`). And its cache key has an open correctness bug, CR-11: the key omits the foreground mask (`reports/code-review-2026-09-30.md:26`). No preset has been visually evaluated either: stills exist for only 5 of 10 presets (`out/lead/deck-parity/style-presets/`), and none of them are the `LOOK_*` presets.

A defensible signature default takes the features that only change the rendered layer and are cheap. It keeps the photo itself untouched, and degrades to classic-like behaviour when inputs are missing.

| Layer | Signature default | Rationale | Source in code |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ridges | `ink` (distance-faded width/opacity) | Biggest single "drawn by a cartographer" cue; composite-only, no per-material port | `src/lib/look/glsl/composite.ts:6-7` |
| Labels | `panorama` layout, tiered weights | Already built and hysteretic; **no preset selects it today** | `src/lib/look/labels/layout.ts:1-16`; only `layout:` in styles is `"classic"` at `src/lib/style/defaults.ts:133` |
| Haze | `LOOK_ATMOSPHERE` physical, analytic airlight (not fitted) | Chromatic, height-integrated haze without the sky model or haze fit | `src/lib/look/atmosphere.ts:1-9` |
| Relief (replace/world) | Swiss relief at low realism, Alpine albedo | Matches what Swiss users expect; sun falls back to fixed NW when EXIF time is absent | `src/lib/look/glsl/relief.ts:1-8`, `src/lib/look/glsl/ramps.ts:1-6` |
| Contours | Keep `fwidth` AA; warm-brown or white instead of the cyan ramp; Tanaka only in replace/world | Cyan-to-magenta reads "technical overlay" | `src/lib/style/defaults.ts:23-40` |
| Output | Neutral on the *replacing layer only* | Already scoped that way in the composite | `src/lib/look/glsl/composite.ts:9, 220-222` |
| Opt-in upgrade | "Match photo" toggle → refine + fitted airlight + harmonize | Pays the 4.5 MB / 50 ms only when asked; ship after CR-11 | `src/lib/style/presets.ts:211-230` |

Two choices in this table need justification. On light direction, the default fixed sun should move from the current NW-ish vector toward NNW. A user study found NNW better than NW, and in ranges whose grain runs NW–SE, NW light parallels the terrain and flattens it ([Stefanakis, EuroCarto 2026](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/12/135/2026/ica-abs-12-135-2026.pdf)). A stronger variant picks the azimuth that maximises cross-slope contrast against the view's aspect histogram. That is a cheap addition to `relief/field.ts`, but nobody has evaluated it. On relief strength, the ETH survey found that texture shading and multidirectional oblique-weighted shading "often overemphasize detail, reducing readability" in mountains, and that a clear-sky model did best for most mountain landforms ([Farmakis-Serebryakova & Hurni, ICC 2025](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/10/73/2025/ica-abs-10-73-2025.pdf)). That supports a restrained Swiss realism setting rather than maximal relief.

### The gate before flipping

The flip itself is an afternoon. The evidence for it is two days. First, render all ten presets plus the candidate default over 20–30 photos from the wild benchmark's dev split, in overlay, replace and world modes. Run a blind pairwise preference with the user and two or three outsiders. Second, fix CR-11. Third, re-capture the style baseline twice: `classic`, which stays frozen, and the new default, which becomes its own pinned baseline. Fourth, make `scripts/demo/export-overlay.ts` and the hero shot pass an explicit `?style=`, so the showcase never silently tracks whatever the default is. Saved users keep their stored preset, because `mt-image.viewStyle.v1` already persists `{preset, overrides}` (`src/lib/style/store.ts:1-5`). Only new users and URLs without a style see the change. Nothing in the notes measures whether users prefer any preset, so this preference test is the missing evidence, not a formality.

## Seven quick wins make overlays belong to the photo

The frontier work on photo/terrain fusion agrees on one point. The distinctive looks come from computing lines and tints from known DEM geometry, then modulating them by the photo's light and haze, rather than painting generic GIS symbology on top. Rigi has metric per-pixel range, normals, a sky mask and a cast-shadow field, so most of this reduces to composite-stage shader work. That matters because every per-material technique must be written four times: three GLSL, deck GLSL through `src/lib/look/glsl/block.ts`, WGSL in `src/lib/deck-webgpu`, and CPU parity. **Composite and screen-space work is therefore the cheapest category in this codebase**, and the list below favours it. Effort figures are judgements by the researchers and this report, not measurements.

| # | Upgrade | Where | Effort | Touches photo pixels? | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Signature default + still/preference gate (above) | `src/lib/style/store.ts`, `presets.ts`, CI baseline | 2–3 d | No | Built features; preference unmeasured |
| 2 | Port the /roll PCF + slope-scaled-bias visibility test into the single-photo drape | `src/lib/materials.ts:480,508`; `src/lib/deck/terrain-layer.ts:536,562`; from `src/lib/roll/map/multi-drape-layer.ts` | 1 d | No (drape is a render of the photo) | WebGPU twin took grazing acne from 17.7 % to 0 % (`src/lib/deck-webgpu/README.md`) |
| 3 | Terrain shadow + haze on overlays (contours, trails, leaders darken in shadow, fade toward airlight with range) | composite/terrain shader using `relief/field.ts` shadows | 1–2 d | No | Inference; no published evaluation |
| 4 | Leader-line occlusion (dash/fade where a leader crosses nearer terrain) | `src/lib/look/labels/PeakLabelsSvg.tsx`, range buffer readback | 0.5–1 d | No | Inference |
| 5 | Grouped sub-summits ("Dom +3" chip, expand on hover) | `src/lib/look/labels/layout.ts`, `rank.ts` | 1 d | No | Grouped labels beat per-item labels on task time |
| 6 | Range-ordered reveal modes: ink-first, then photo fills in near to far; wireframe→photo sweep | `src/lib/reveal/config.ts`, `src/lib/reveal/glsl.ts` | 1 d | Only during animation | Inference; fits existing arrival field |
| 7 | Landing hygiene: re-export hero/overlay shots in the signature look, render the existing reveal video, pick a display face, drop dead template CSS, check caption contrast | `src/routes/index.tsx`, `src/styles.css:1, 9-28, 213-224`, `scripts/demo/reveal-video.ts` | 1 d | No | Audit-verified dead code |

Item 2 is the clearest debt. The shipped single-photo drape still uses a one-texel binary visibility test, `r < seen * 1.015 + 15.0`. It appears in both engines: `src/lib/materials.ts:480` and `src/lib/deck/terrain-layer.ts:536`, which this report checked directly. Meanwhile, the /roll multi-drape and the WebGPU drape already carry a 2×2 PCF vote with slope-scaled bias. Speckled photo edges on grazing slopes are the kind of artefact that makes a precise registration look sloppy.

Item 3 is the cheapest way to stop overlays looking like a sticker. The fusion research calls terrain-cast shadows on overlays "the single cheapest cue that makes vector overlays look like they are 'in' the photo". It pairs that with applying the same aerial perspective to overlay colour, `mix(overlay, A, 1 − exp(−βr))`. Rigi already computes both inputs. Contours already have a distance fade (4–25 km to a 0.3 floor, `src/lib/style/defaults.ts:23-40`), but it is an opacity ramp, not a colour shift toward the haze. This is a proposal synthesised from NPR fundamentals. No paper evaluates it on registered mountain photos.

Item 5 has the best empirical support of the label ideas. In a 2025 AR clutter study, "Situated Grouped" labels cut completion time from 16.5 s to 14.0 s at equal accuracy (about 91–92 %) ([Park et al., arXiv 2507.00198](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00198v1)). Massifs like Monte Rosa or the Bernese Oberland triplets are exactly that clutter case.

On halos, AR legibility studies favour white text on a billboard backing or with a thin outline ([JGED review](https://sp.ftn.uns.ac.rs/index.php/jged/article/view/2719)). A frosted, photo-sampled pill gated by local contrast satisfies that without an opaque box. It is a reasonable successor to the current `drop-shadow(0 1px 3px)` halo. The specific frosted treatment is a design inference, not a tested result.

Two candidates from the notes do not belong on the quick-win list. One note suggests upgrading deck to 9.4 for analytic line antialiasing ([deck.gl What's New](https://deck.gl/docs/whats-new)). But the vendored 9.4.0-beta.4 `PathLayer` already exposes an `antialiasing` prop (`node_modules/@deck.gl/layers/dist/path-layer/path-layer.d.ts:49`), and **nothing in `src/` uses PathLayer or TextLayer**: contours, ridges and trails are drawn inside the terrain and composite shaders. So that gain is mostly moot unless overlays move to vector layers. The second candidate is AgX tone mapping in place of Neutral. It remains unadopted and is low value: Neutral already ships under `LOOK_OUTPUT` (`src/lib/engine.ts:263-322`). The real gap is that render-only and world modes in classic have no tone mapping at all (`src/lib/engine.ts:545-585`), and the signature default closes that.

## Bigger bets belong in the world view, the landing page and opt-in art modes

The larger techniques split cleanly by whether they can live in the photo-matched view. Geometry-distorting and photo-altering techniques cannot be the default there. They are the right tools for the 3D world view, the /roll map, the landing page and shareable "art renders".

**A palette-texture material for Berann and seasonal looks (about 1–2 weeks including tuning).** The best-documented real-time painterly terrain system is still Brown, Samavati & Sousa's "Real-Time Panorama Maps" ([Expressive/NPAR 2017](https://giv.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/pdf/rtpanoramamaps-expressive2017-brown.pdf)). It replaces the lighting model with per-class cel palettes indexed by *signed* n·l, so shade sides take complementary hues: brown rock turns blue, green forest turns violet. It also uses a haze that shifts hue toward the sky while preserving value contrast, darkens the bottom 20–25 % of the frame, and adds tree brush strokes, all at 23 fps on 2015 hardware. Rigi's `berann` preset today is an absolute-elevation ramp plus ink (`src/lib/style/presets.ts:257`), so it falls well short of that look. In Rigi, the class would come from DEM-only signals: slope over roughly 40° is cliff, high and gentle is snow, low is forest, with an aspect-raised snowline. That is a small extension of the existing Alpine albedo in `src/lib/look/glsl/ramps.ts`. One mechanism (a 6×256 palette texture plus light settings) then covers Berann, golden hour and Woodruff-style seasonal grades ([Woodruff, seasonal relief](https://andywoodruff.com/blog/seasonal-relief/)). The class thresholds are unsourced engineering defaults and need tuning against Rigi's photos. Berann's quadratic terrain bend and Jenny's progressive projection ([Jenny et al. 2010](https://mail.colororacle.org/berniejenny/pdf/2010_Jenny_etal_ProgressiveProjection.pdf)) belong only in orbit and landing views, never in a matched view.

**A photo-to-3D projective camera tween (about 1 week).** Apple's Look Around transitions feel better than Street View's because they use real intermediate frames and parallax instead of blur ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/19/06/07/hands-on-with-ios-13-look-around-in-apple-maps)). Rigi can do something no peak app does, because it knows the solved pose. It would start at the exact photo camera with the photo projected on the DEM and tween position and FOV toward an oblique map pose over about 1.2 s. Per fragment, it would blend from photo texture to orthophoto by the angle between the current view ray and the photo ray, gated by photo-camera visibility. This turns the registration, Rigi's core technical win, into a visible "snap into place" moment. PeakVisor still relies on manual drag-to-align ([PeakVisor tutorial](https://peakvisor.com/tutorial_en.html)). The drape shader, world view and the shared camera modes (`?cammodes`, `src/lib/flags/index.ts`) already exist, and item 2's PCF port is a prerequisite. No published study of photo↔3D transition aesthetics was found, so this rests on product judgement.

**DEM-driven parallax and depth of field for the landing page and share videos (about 3–5 days).** Rigi's DEM depth is metric and occlusion-correct, unlike the estimated depth in the 3D Ken Burns pipeline ([Niklaus et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05483v1)) or iOS 26 Spatial Scenes ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/06/10/psa-spatial-scenes-will-work-on-any-iphone-running-ios-26/)). The method is to clamp parallax so the nearest DEM range moves under about 2–3 % of frame width, then fill disocclusions behind ridges by stretching sky or farther terrain. Depth of field can follow BokehMe's logic of a classical renderer that is softened only at known discontinuities ([arXiv 2206.12614](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12614)). Rigi's sky mask and Step Inside foreground masks mark those discontinuities. Both effects move or blur photo pixels, so they belong to motion and export surfaces, not the interactive matched view.

**Landing-page choreography (about 3–5 days).** The verifiable exemplars share four moves: scroll-bound camera keyframes authored on a timeline ([Codrops/Theatre.js](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=70449)), staged loading reveals, classic cartography rendered crisply in WebGL ([National Geographic awards entry](https://awards.journalists.org/entries/what-is-the-tallest-mountain-on-earth/)), and one bespoke animation per concept. San Rita builds its terrain from real heightmaps and loads it in stages ([Abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/node/89189)). Rigi's landing (`src/routes/index.tsx:51-264`) already has the right sections and respects the no-scroll-trap rules. What it lacks is a staged hero (DEM mesh, then relief, then drape, then labels) and a keyframed camera for `LiveRollMap`. Rigi should take the patterns, not the R3F/GSAP stack, since the engine is deck. Mapbox Standard's Day/Dusk/Dawn/Night presets show that light presets are the cheapest mood lever ([Geo Week News](https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/mapbox-standard-core-style-3d-urban-map/)). Rigi knows each photo's time, so "golden hour" and "blue hour" fall out of the palette material above.

**Texture shading and hachures (experimental, 1–3 days each).** Leland Brown's texture shading is a fractional Laplacian with α around 0.8, clamped to the 1st–99th percentile, with an FIR approximation for tiles ([texshade-py](https://github.com/fasiha/texshade-py)). It could feed the existing relief field as a structure layer blended at 0.3 or less. That cap follows the ETH over-detail finding above. LIC-based hachures follow the Czech state-map work ([Kyncl & Lysák, EuroCarto 2024](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/7/81/2024/ica-abs-7-81-2024.pdf)), but no browser implementation was found. Treat both as world-view and print-style experiments. Neural Eduard shading has no public weights ([Stefanakis](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/12/135/2026/ica-abs-12-135-2026.pdf)), so it is not an option for now.

**Art modes that alter the photo (each 1–3 weeks, opt-in only).** These are the most spectacular items on the list, and the riskiest. Shading-ratio relighting multiplies the photo by new DEM shading over fitted old shading, clamped and guided-filtered. It is a WebGL-feasible, licence-clean analogue of Careaga & Aksoy's path-trace-plus-neural pipeline ([arXiv 2508.05626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05626)), but it is an untested inference that will fail on cloud shadows and snow albedo. Sky replacement is seam-free here because the sky mask and sun are known. It would also need the distant-terrain airlight re-fitted to the new horizon colour, which commercial tools only approximate ([Skylum Sky AI](https://support.skylum.com/editing-tools/landscape-tools/sky-ai?c_url=3)). A physically based sky would mean upgrading the current "Preetham-like" gradient (`src/lib/look/glsl/atmosphere.ts:84-87`). takram's MIT atmosphere is the best open stack ([three-geospatial](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial)), but it is three-only and mid-rewrite to WebGPU/TSL. With deck as the default renderer, porting the Hillaire LUT approach into `src/lib/look` beats taking the dependency. NPR stylisation also fits here: anisotropic Kuwahara ([Kyprianidis et al.](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v28i7pp1955-1963)), watercolour, halftone, and the "depth-sliced bands" woodblock poster look. All of it is real-time in WebGL2, and none of it may run on the photo in the matched view.

**Engine bets to defer.** deck.gl 9.4 runs every official layer on WebGPU but still calls it "not yet recommended for production" ([deck.gl What's New](https://deck.gl/docs/whats-new)). The three.js TSL post stack (TRAA, GTAO, bloom, LUT, film) is first-party but needs every custom GLSL material rewritten ([three.js TSL wiki](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/wiki/Three.js-Shading-Language)). Both point the same way: the WebGL2 deck default stays, and `src/lib/deck-webgpu` stays the lab path. A minimal premium post chain (LUT grade, vignette, grain) can be built as luma passes in the existing composite instead. Spark 2.0 streams 100M+ splats over WebGL2, including iOS ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-previews-spark-2.0)). That is relevant to the iOS gap in roadmap L3 (`reports/roadmap.md:91`). But it is three-based, while Step Inside currently uses a deck EWA splat layer (`src/lib/nearfield/deck-splat-layer.ts`). Adopting it is an architecture decision, not an aesthetic quick win.

## Photo integrity and licences set hard limits on the frontier

Rigi's photo is not decoration. It is the evidence that the match is right. That turns several frontier techniques from "nice look" into "misleading". Four integrity rules follow from the audit and the fusion research. They should become part of the style schema.

The first rule covers geometry: **no geometric distortion in any matched view.** Berann bend, plan-oblique shift ([Jenny & Patterson](https://cartographicperspectives.org/index.php/journal/article/view/cp57-jenny-patterson)), progressive projection, vertical exaggeration and landmark exaggeration are allowed only in world, orbit and landing views. They must never reach the overlay or replace pipelines.

The second rule covers photo pixels, which fall into three tiers. Tier 0 changes only the render layer: everything in the signature default, plus harmonize, which moves the *layer* toward the photo (`src/lib/look/glsl/composite.ts:8`). Tier 1 is imperceptible and global. `LOOK_OUTPUT`'s ±1 LSB dither currently runs "everywhere", photo included (`src/lib/look/glsl/composite.ts:232`). That is acceptable, but it should be documented, and it should be off for any measurement export. Tier 2 visibly alters the photo: relighting, sky replacement, NPR, depth of field, parallax and diffusion repaints. Tier 2 must be explicitly labelled, never default, excluded from eval and benchmark renders, and paired with an always-available "original" toggle.

The third rule covers lines: **no snapping that hides pose error.** The fusion notes propose snapping ink lines to photo edges within ±2 px because this "hides residual pose error". It would raise perceived quality, but it would also fake accuracy that Rigi's whole value proposition claims to measure. If built, it needs a debug toggle and must never run in evaluation. The existing `LOOK_INK` skyline snaps to the *refined coverage mask*, which is derived from the photo's own sky segmentation (`src/lib/look/glsl/composite.ts:6-7`). That is a softer version of the same trade-off and deserves the same disclosure.

The fourth rule keeps appearance renders out of matching. Date-matched appearance renders gave no matching gain, and a flat snow tint "wipes out rock texture" (`reports/negative-results.md:55`). Aesthetic looks are display-only and must not feed the matcher.

### What remains unverified

The notes' uncertainty is uneven, and some of it matters for these decisions. No preset, including the proposed default, has a measured quality or preference result. The audit inferred on-screen appearance from code, not screenshots. The overlay-shadow, leader-occlusion, frosted-halo, shading-ratio relighting, range-ordered reveal and projective-tween proposals are syntheses with no published evaluation on registered mountain photos. The DEM class thresholds and seasonal palette values are unsourced defaults. The exemplar survey could not verify any 2023–2026 NYT, Reuters, WaPo or Bloomberg terrain pieces. Typography used by PeakFinder, PeakVisor and Apple is undocumented, so the label-typeface choice (Manrope versus the unused Fraunces, or a condensed humanist face) is an open design call rather than a research-backed one. Mobile and iOS performance budgets for any look feature are unmeasured. All bench numbers come from one Apple-Silicon Mac (`reports/deck-default.md:7-12`).

## Conclusion

The frontier has moved less than Rigi has. The documented 2023–2026 state of the art is mostly what Rigi's look modules already implement: neural Swiss shading productised, MapLibre and MapTiler shipping multidirectional coloured hillshade, a 2017 paper still defining real-time Berann rendering, and premium map SDKs defined by light presets and LUT grades. Where Rigi goes beyond that frontier, it does so because it alone knows the photo's pose, time and per-pixel metric depth. The haze fit, the cast shadows and the ink are all display uses of that knowledge. So the leverage now is editorial: choose a default, measure it, and protect the photo while doing so. Rigi's distinctiveness comes from geometric truth, so a look that bends or repaints the matched photo undercuts the product even when it looks better.

The sequence that follows is short. Ship the signature default after a two-day preference pass and the CR-11 fix. Spend a week on overlay-belonging quick wins and the drape PCF port.
