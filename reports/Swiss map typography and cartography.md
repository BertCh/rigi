# Swiss map lettering is a rule system, not a font

Swiss map typography is a small, strict rule system: **type posture encodes meaning, size encodes importance, and colour matches the symbol being named.** The Swiss Style (Ruder, Müller-Brockmann) contributes the restraint, a few sans-serif faces and clear hierarchy, but its page-grid rules do not transfer to labels placed against geography. The label rules come from Eduard Imhof's 1962 essay "Die Anordnung der Namen in der Karte" (English 1975) and are now encoded in swisstopo's open vector styles. The main change in the lineage is the **2014 Landeskarte redesign, which replaced the in-house LK roman and italic lettering with Frutiger**, so swisstopo's digital maps now use a Frutiger Neue family: italic for nature, condensed upright for settlements, tracked capitals for administrative names. Placement is NP-hard in general, but production systems (Mapbox/MapLibre, deck.gl) use greedy priority-ordered collision tests, while labelling peaks in a panorama or photo has its own best-studied formulation, boundary labelling in rows above the skyline with vertical leaders. For Rigi, the practical result is a role table (below) taken from swisstopo's published style, a candidate-position and row-stacking placement scheme for map and photo labels, and a font decision that avoids unlicensed Frutiger glyphs. Rigi's existing OFL Fira Sans and Fira Sans Condensed already cover most of the roles. Several claims below remain unverified, and a gaps list closes the report. The broader Swiss cartographic canon (relief, rock, colour, furniture) is in [swiss-cartography-review.md](swiss-cartography-review.md) and [gipfelbuch-design-book.md](gipfelbuch-design-book.md); this report goes deeper on lettering only.

## Swiss Style gives restraint and hierarchy, not label rules

The International Typographic Style emphasises "simplicity, clarity, readability, and objectivity", with grids, sans-serif faces (Helvetica, Univers, Akzidenz-Grotesk), flush-left ragged-right text and minimal ornament ([Wikipedia: International Typographic Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Typographic_Style)). Its canonical texts are Emil Ruder's *Typographie* (1967, 19 chapters on texture, weight, colour, legibility, spacing and leading) ([Wikipedia: Emil Ruder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Ruder)) and Müller-Brockmann's *Grid Systems*, which builds hierarchical modular grids of 8 to 32 fields ([Thames & Hudson](https://thamesandhudson.com.au/?p=93182)). The research could not retrieve numeric rules from these books (size steps, line length, leading), so none are asserted here. What carries over to maps is hierarchy by size and weight, a very small palette of faces, and objectivity. What does not carry over is the page grid and flush-left setting, because labels sit against terrain, and map lettering routinely breaks Swiss Style norms with italics for water, letter-spaced area names, curved baselines and coloured text (inference from the notes).

The typefaces form three layers. Neue Haas Grotesk (1956-57, Münchenstein; renamed Helvetica in 1959) and Univers (1957) are the neo-grotesque pillars ([Wikipedia: Helvetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetica); [Wikipedia: Univers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univers)). Adrian Frutiger's humanist **Frutiger** (1976) grew from airport signage and aims at "total clarity", with wide apertures and a high x-height ([Wikipedia: Frutiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frutiger_(typeface))). Road and rail signage show the same priorities as map lettering. The SNV face (1972) was replaced on Swiss road signs by ASTRA-Frutiger in 2003, a condensed cut with widened ascender/descender spacing, so long names fit without losing legibility ([Wikipedia: SNV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNV_(typeface)); [swissinfo](https://swissinfo.ch/eng/culture/switzerland-still-a-font-of-creativity/41628070)). SBB's 1978-80 station system by Müller-Brockmann used Helvetica ([eguide.ch](https://www.eguide.ch/en/objekt/sbb/)). The practical reading is that **Frutiger is the "official Swiss" map face and Helvetica-type neo-grotesques are the "Swiss Style" graphic face**; open-source humanist sans such as Fira Sans are closer to the former.

## swisstopo lettering moved from bespoke roman and italic to Frutiger in 2014

Production history is documented, but the printed typefaces of the early maps are not. The Dufour map (1:100,000, 1845-1864) was intaglio-engraved ([Wikipedia: Dufour Map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dufour_Map)); the Siegfried map was copper-engraved at 1:25,000 in black, brown and blue and lithographed for the 1:50,000 Alpine sheets ([swisstopo](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/en/siegfried-map)). The research found **no source for the actual lettering styles of Dufour or Siegfried sheets**, so none are claimed. The Landeskarte (ordered 1935; 1:50,000 from 1938, 1:25,000 from 1952) is better documented. The 2008 legend PDF, inspected directly for its embedded fonts, uses the in-house families **LKRoemisch (cuts A0 to F10; Fett, LiegendFett, Mager) and LKKursiv**, plus Frutiger LT Pro for the legend text itself ([Zeichenerklärung 2008](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/WxsMJ4yE7xeV/Zeichenerklärung_2008_d.pdf)). The reading of "Römisch" as upright roman and "Liegend" as slanted roman is an interpretation of the font names.

The 2008 legend shows the class system precisely. Script depends on object type: political communes are upright, dependent places and quarters slanted, valleys and mountains in normal weight, areas in light weight, and settlement size follows population (Bern, over 50,000 inhabitants, in capitals at 29 pt; a hamlet in italic at 14 pt; a single hut in bold italic at 10 pt). Water is blue (about #37A0DF in the PDF), with large rivers and lakes in capitals or slanted roman and small rivers and glaciers in italic, so "all water is italic" is not strictly true on the legacy sheet. Forests are letter-spaced light italic; glaciers use abbreviations (Gl., Gh., Vadret); abbreviations are listed in four languages ([Zeichenerklärung 2008](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/WxsMJ4yE7xeV/Zeichenerklärung_2008_d.pdf)).

From April 2015 the new Landeskarte (first Jungfrau sheets; all 247 sheets planned by 2019) adopted **Frutiger for labelling, replacing the face used since 1952**, together with violet boundaries, red rail and graded road fills ([plattformj.ch](https://www.plattformj.ch/artikel/136300/); [swisstopo ICA abstract](https://kartographie.geo.tu-dresden.de/downloads/ica-gen/symposium2015/20151203_ext_abstract_swisstopo.pdf)). The redesign also moved to the official survey spelling, with tendentially more names ([DGPF 2014](https://dgpf.de/src/tagung/jt2014/proceedings/proceedings/papers/Beitrag163.pdf)). It was criticised: a type-design paper calls it the first time a sans-serif appears on Swiss maps and regrets the choice given Swiss typographic culture ([Biniek et al. 2018](https://ica-proc.copernicus.org/articles/1/9/2018/ica-proc-1-9-2018.pdf)); the "first time" claim comes from outside authors and is not confirmed by swisstopo. Names follow the Weisungen 2011: local pronunciation for local names, customary spelling for nationally important features such as major mountains, glaciers and passes, and standard German for building names ([Weisungen 2011](https://www.cadastre-manual.admin.ch/dam/it/sd-web/4SE6MyxeDpLv/Weisungen-geografische-Namen-de.pdf)). The source dataset swissNAMES3D holds over 490,000 names ([swisstopo](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/en/landscape-model-swissnames3d)).

## Today's reference is swisstopo's open vector style: italic for nature, condensed for people

The most precise and current evidence is swisstopo's own style JSON, downloaded and parsed on 2026-10-02: Basiskarte v1.26.0 (88 layers), Light v1.19.0 and Imagery v1.19.0, all using only a Frutiger Neue family (Regular, Italic, Medium, Condensed Regular/Medium/Bold) served from a self-hosted glyph endpoint ([basemap style](https://vectortiles.geo.admin.ch/styles/ch.swisstopo.basemap.vt/style.json); [light](https://vectortiles.geo.admin.ch/styles/ch.swisstopo.lightbasemap.vt/style.json); [imagery](https://vectortiles.geo.admin.ch/styles/ch.swisstopo.imagerybasemap.vt/style.json)). The roles are consistent. **Peaks are italic in dark navy rgb(27,36,62), tracking 0.025 em, name plus elevation on a second line at 0.75 font scale, size 12 to 23 px by class.** Water, glaciers and waterways are italic in blue rgb(47,134,188) with positive tracking (0.05-0.1 em for lakes, 0.15-0.3 for glacier lines, 0.1 for waterways, up to 0.8 em for valleys). Cities are Condensed Medium in near-black rgb(32,32,32), tracking 0.029; towns and villages are condensed; country and boundary names are uppercase in magenta rgb(195,85,146) with 0.12-0.2 em tracking; rail names are red; parks Medium green; contour and spot-height numerals italic, brown on land and blue on water and ice ([basemap style](https://vectortiles.geo.admin.ch/styles/ch.swisstopo.basemap.vt/style.json)).

Halos are faint on the light map: colour rgba(242,242,242,0.9), blur 0.25, width growing from 0.5 px at z12 to about 1 to 1.25 px at z14-16, with light-blue halos on water labels. On the imagery basemap the logic inverts to near-white text with dark halos (width 1.5 for places) ([imagery style](https://vectortiles.geo.admin.ch/styles/ch.swisstopo.imagerybasemap.vt/style.json)). That inversion is directly relevant to photo overlays. Background is rgb(253,253,254), the yellow light tone is only 4% opacity, hachure and scree are pre-vectorised polygons, and the data carries `name:de/fr/it/rm/latin` while the styles label with `name:latin` only. ETH's Neumann describes "selective masking" behind labels over dark hachure in QGIS, an alternative to heavy halos ([QGIS UC 2026](https://talks.osgeo.org/qgis-uc2026/talk/P7EBXR/)). swisstopo's own roadmap says the next-generation vector map "deliberately abandons previous principles of the classic national map in favour of performance and legibility" and plans AI-derived terrain ([ICC 2023](https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/6/274/2023/ica-abs-6-274-2023.pdf)), so in practice the Swiss look is now a data layer plus a rules-driven type layer. MapTiler helped build the styles with "color codes, fonts, and symbols inspired by official Swiss cartography" ([MapTiler](https://maptiler.com/news/2020/08/swisstopo-vector-tiles-development)).

There is one licensing trap. The data is open under swisstopo's OGD terms ([swisstopo](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/en/web-maps-base-map)), but Frutiger is a Monotype trademark and Neue Frutiger is a commercial family ([Wikipedia: Frutiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frutiger_(typeface))). Whether the glyph PBFs may be reused by third parties is **unverified**; equating swisstopo's "Frutiger Neue" with Monotype's Neue Frutiger is also an inference. Rigi should copy the style roles, not the glyph files.

## Label placement: Imhof's rules, greedy collision, and rows with leaders for panoramas

Imhof's six principles are legibility and locatability, clear association with the object, no overlap or concealment, names that reveal extent and importance, a typographic hierarchy that mirrors the classification, and names neither crowded nor evenly scattered (via secondary summaries: [UNBC lecture](https://gis.unbc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lettering2025.pdf); [Kern & Brewer 2008](https://cartographicperspectives.org/index.php/journal/article/download/cp60-kern-brewer/pdf/1397)). The per-geometry rules are widely repeated. Point labels try eight positions, ranked upper-right, lower-right, upper-left, lower-left (Yoeli 1972, as summarised by Kern & Brewer; Imhof himself said only to begin the name to the right of the symbol). Line labels follow a relatively straight stretch above the line and clear of descenders, are not letter-spaced but repeated at intervals, and stay readable (not past vertical, with the Imhof exception that contour numerals point uphill). Area labels sit inside the area, are letter-spaced evenly but by no more than about **4x letter height**, curve by no more than about **60 degrees**, and fall back to a point label if the area is too small. Hierarchy comes from size, case, weight and colour, with higher contrast between classes than within them, and few typefaces with varied form instead ([UNBC lecture](https://gis.unbc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/lettering2025.pdf)). All of this rests on **teaching-note and review summaries; Imhof 1975 and Yoeli 1972 were not read**, so numbers are indicative.

Automation reduces to a cost-ranked candidate set. Point-feature label placement is NP-hard ([Christensen, Marks & Shieber 1995](https://www.merl.com/publications/docs/TR94-12.pdf)), and commercial engines work around that with weights and fitting strategies: Maplex uses 0-1000 feature weights and strategies such as stacking and abbreviation ([Esri](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/3.5/help/mapping/text/label-with-the-maplex-label-engine.htm)), and placed about 7% more labels than the standard engine, about 93% with no overlap ([Kern & Brewer](https://cartographicperspectives.org/index.php/journal/article/download/cp60-kern-brewer/pdf/1397)). The interactive standard is greedy: Mapbox GL processes symbols in importance order against a grid index, treating point labels as rectangles and line labels as chains of circles, with cross-tile IDs so opacity fades keep placement stable ([Collision Detection wiki](https://github.com/mapbox/mapbox-gl-native/wiki/Collision-Detection)); MapLibre adds `text-variable-anchor` for ordered anchor tries ([Mapbox guide](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/dive-deeper/optimize-map-label-placement/)). deck.gl's `CollisionFilterExtension` runs on the GPU with `getCollisionPriority` (-1000 to 1000, higher wins) and collision groups, but cannot try alternate anchors, so a CPU pass must supply candidates ([deck.gl docs](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/extensions/collision-filter-extension)). deck.gl text outlines are SDF-only, with `outlineWidth` relative to font size and optional background boxes ([TextLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/layers/text-layer)). Single-channel SDF rounds corners at small sizes (secondary sources), so small Swiss-style labels need a large atlas or a floor around 10-12 px (inference).

For panoramas and photo overlays the relevant formulation is **boundary labelling**: Gemsa, Haunert and Nöllenburg annotate panorama peaks with rectangular labels in k rows above the image, each joined by a vertical leader that crosses no other label; "MinRow" minimises rows, "MaxWeight" maximises total weight in k rows, and sliding the label along its leader is allowed ([ACM GIS 2011](https://www1.pub.informatik.uni-wuerzburg.de/pub/haunert/pdf/GemsaEtAl2011.pdf)). Two-sided labelling is NP-hard ([Gemsa et al.](https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/ghn-mrbla-14.pdf)). Bell, Feiner and Höllerer's view management adjusts position, size or transparency to keep projected labels from occluding each other ([Columbia](https://graphics.cs.columbia.edu/projects/ViewManagement)); its algorithm details were not extracted. **PeakFinder's algorithm, ETH's 3D-atlas labelling and Heim/Berann/Imhof panorama lettering conventions were not found in any citable source.**

## Implications for Rigi

**What exists (verified in the repo).** Peak labels live in `src/lib/look/labels/` (`rank.ts`, `layout.ts`, `canvas.ts`, `PeakLabelsSvg.tsx`, `useLabelFonts.ts`). `rank.ts` ranks by prominence x 3 + elevation - range x 0.012 and runs a greedy declutter in frame units (about 7 px per character) shared by both engines. Label font defaults to `"Fira Sans, system-ui, sans-serif"` (`src/lib/style/defaults.ts`). Self-hosted OFL fonts are in `public/fonts/gipfelbuch/` (Fira Sans 300-600 plus italic 400, Fira Sans Condensed 500/600, Fira Mono, Source Serif 4, hand faces), recorded in `public/fonts/gipfelbuch/LICENSES.md` with the Fira reserved-name note. The Gipfelbuch type scale is in `src/components/gipfelbuch/swiss/type.ts`. I did not audit how the map engines render place names, so the recommendations below are targets, not a diff.

**1. Type roles.** Adopt swisstopo's role split using Fira Sans (humanist, close in spirit to Frutiger) and Fira Sans Condensed. Values for "swisstopo" come from the parsed style (verified); the rest is a recommendation.

| Feature class | Style / weight | Case | Colour (light ground) | Tracking | Halo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak name (map and photo) | Italic, Regular (swisstopo Italic) | Mixed | #1B243E (swisstopo rgb 27,36,62) | +0.025 em | ~1 px, ground colour at 90% |
| Peak elevation | Same face, 0.75 scale, second line | Numerals | as peak | +0.025 em | as peak |
| Alpine / main peak vs minor | Same style, larger size (12 to 23 px by class) | Mixed | as peak | same | same |
| Pass, hut, minor point | Upright Regular (or italic if natural; legacy LK used italic) | Mixed | near-black #202020 | 0 to +0.02 em | ~0.75 px |
| Lake / sea | Italic | Mixed, capitals for the largest | #2F86BC | +0.05 to +0.1 em, grows with size | light blue #D2EEFF, ~0.5 px |
| River | Italic, along line | Mixed | #2F86BC | +0.1 em | light blue, 0.9 alpha |
| Glacier | Italic, area label inside | Mixed, abbreviation Gl. when short | #2F86BC | +0.15 to +0.3 em | light blue |
| Valley / range / massif | Italic or spaced capitals, along axis | Capitals for ranges | #202020 | up to 0.8 em for valleys, never more than 4x letter height | ~1 px |
| City | Condensed Medium | Mixed (legacy LK used capitals for large cities) | #202020 | +0.03 em | 1 to 1.25 px |
| Town, village, hamlet | Condensed Regular, size by population | Mixed | #202020 | +0.01 to +0.025 em | 1 px |
| Country / canton / boundary | Condensed Regular | UPPERCASE | #C35592 (rgb 195,85,146) | +0.12 to +0.2 em | 1 px |
| Contour / spot elevation | Italic, numerals | Numerals | brown #AB7E40 land, blue on water/ice, grey on scree | +0.1 em | masked, thin |
| On photo or imagery ground | Same posture, near-white fill | as above | #FFFFFA | as above | dark halo (swisstopo imagery uses 1.5 px, rgba 48,48,48) |

Fira Sans Condensed is currently bundled only in weights 500 and 600; a Regular cut would be needed for the town and village role (verify availability). Fira Sans has no condensed italic, so italic roles use Fira Sans Italic 400 (the only italic bundled), and a Medium or SemiBold italic would need adding.

**2. Map label placement rules.** (a) Rank by class first, then elevation/prominence, then population (the existing `peakRank` formula is a good seed), with a hysteresis bonus for already-placed labels to prevent flicker while panning (inference from the Mapbox design). (b) For each point label, try the ordered candidate set NE, SE, NW, SW, then E, W, N, S, with a cost per rank and a halo-inclusive box; take the first free position, otherwise drop the label (Yoeli/Imhof ordering, secondary). (c) Test collisions against a spatial grid with greedy insertion; do candidate choice on the CPU and use `CollisionFilterExtension` only for per-frame pruning during camera moves. (d) Area names: horizontal if they fit, else along the principal axis with total bend of at most 60 degrees and tracking of at most 4x letter height; if the area is too small, convert to a point label. (e) Line names: follow a straight stretch above the line, repeat rather than spread, keep text upright. (f) Keep small labels at 10 to 12 px or more under SDF rendering, or raise the atlas `fontSize`; MSDF is an option if corners round (unverified for deck.gl).

**3. Photo-overlay peak labels.** (a) Project peaks and drop any hidden by a DEM line-of-sight test. (b) Weight by prominence, elevation and distance. (c) Place name plus elevation in rows above the skyline with vertical leaders ending at the summit pixel, solving MaxWeight; a greedy "highest weight takes the lowest free row" is a simple approximation to Gemsa et al., and row count k should be capped so the stack never covers the photo subject. (d) Allow limited horizontal sliding of the label along the leader to avoid label-label overlap. (e) Scale or fade labels with distance, and draw nearer labels on top. (f) On photographs use the imagery-style inversion (near-white fill, dark halo about 1.5 px) and choose halo versus fill from the local luminance behind the label (the repo's `src/lib/look/labels/contrast.ts` appears to address contrast; I did not read it). (g) Use Imhof-style posture even here: italic peak names, italic blue water, upright place names.

**4. Fonts.** Candidates, with licences and status:

| Candidate | Role | Licence | Status |

| Source Sans 3 | Alternative humanist, weights and italics, no condensed | OFL 1.1 | Licence from [Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/source-sans-3/about) via search snippet; not verified in the font file |

| Noto Sans | Broad coverage, width axis | OFL | Width axis from memory; unverified |
| Nimbus Sans / TeX Gyre Heros | Helvetica clones | GPL (Nimbus, per Wikipedia); GUST font licence (Heros, unverified) | Avoid in an embedded app unless the licence is checked |
| Hanken Grotesk, Schibsted Grotesk | Contemporary grotesques | Unverified | Licence unconfirmed; check before use |

Test any chosen family on a string covering the Swiss diacritics (a e i o u with grave, acute, circumflex, diaeresis; c-cedilla; oe ligature), since per-font Romansh coverage was not checked. If a MapLibre-style glyph pipeline is ever adopted, PBF glyphs are derived from the font and must respect its licence ([build_pbf_glyphs](https://docs.rs/build_pbf_glyphs)); deck.gl's TextLayer uses an atlas via `fontSettings`, not PBF.

**5. Names.** Prefer `name:latin`-style single labels as swisstopo does, store the language variants, and keep nationally important names in their customary spelling (Weisungen Art. 5). Bilingual labelling (Biel/Bienne) was not researched.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that Swiss lettering is better described as a classification-to-style mapping than a typeface choice: even the 2014 shift from LK roman and italic to Frutiger left the mapping intact, because swisstopo's current vector styles still encode nature as italic, settlements as upright condensed and administration as tracked capitals. That makes the look reproducible in an open font if Rigi copies the roles, tracking and faint halos, and cheap to get wrong if it copies only the face. The one decision that deserves explicit sign-off is not using swisstopo's Frutiger glyphs.

For photo overlays, Swiss cartography supplies the typography but not the algorithm. The usable method comes from the boundary-labelling literature (rows above the skyline, vertical leaders, weight-maximising row assignment), and the Swiss contribution is the posture and colour system laid on top. That pairing, not any published Swiss panorama rulebook, is the real design target for Rigi.

## Research gaps and unverified claims

- Imhof's 1962/1975 text, Yoeli (1972), Robinson, Brewer and Tyner were not read; Imhof/Yoeli rules (eight-position order, 60 degrees, 4x letter height, size-step ratios) come from secondary teaching notes and reviews.
- No source on the typefaces of Dufour or Siegfried lettering; who designed LKRoemisch/LKKursiv, and whether 1952-2013 sheets were engraved, stencilled or phototypeset, is unknown.
- Whether print Landeskarte uses Frutiger LT Pro, Frutiger Neue or a custom cut is unverified; the new-LK per-class size table was not found; the "1952" start date and "first sans-serif on Swiss maps" claim are secondary.
- Glyph PBF reuse terms for swisstopo's Frutiger Neue are unverified.
- Numeric Swiss Style rules (grid values, size scales, leading) were not sourced; the Wanderwege sign typeface, current SBB typeface and current federal CD font are unconfirmed.
- No sourced halo width best practice beyond swisstopo's own style (Esri blog blocked); the maplibre defaults for `text-max-angle` and `text-padding` were not retrieved.
- No source for PeakFinder's algorithm, ETH 3D-atlas label placement, or Berann/Heim panorama lettering; the Bell et al. algorithm details were not extracted.
- SchweizMobil, SAC, Hallwag, FATMAP and Swiss Map Mobile typography not found.
- Per-font licence details for Source Sans 3, Hind, Inter, Hanken Grotesk, Schibsted Grotesk, TeX Gyre Heros and Noto width axes, plus Romansh glyph coverage, were not verified from font files. Fira Sans Condensed Regular and Fira Sans italic weights beyond those in `public/fonts/gipfelbuch/` were not checked.
- None of the recommendations has been rendered or tested in a browser (browser-unverified under the repo's testing policy).
