STEP INSIDE P2: RELATIVE EYE REFINEMENT FOR ROLL SPOTS                                        2026-09-29
=====================================================================================================
Question: P2 missed its gate. Rigi's rotations are good, but the GPS eyes are off by 7-37 m, so near fields
from different photos don't line up. If we pull the eyes of overlapping photos into agreement using image
matches, does the near field of one photo reproject onto the other?

Short answer: we found one real, robust correction. IMG_7059 and IMG_7063 were taken from the same spot:
the solved baseline is 0.15-0.4 m in every variant, while GPS puts them 7.1 m apart. Correcting it
improves registration, but only a little. Coverage of the target's near-field mask goes from ~0-2 % to
3-8 %. PSNR on the covered pixels rises by 6.5 dB. Even so, it stays BELOW a flat mean-colour predictor.
The method as specified (rotations fixed, 2-DoF direction + anchored scale) passes NO pair on this spot.
The implemented variant is a metric 3-DoF solve with rotation/focal nuisances. The evidence is n = 1
usable pair at one viewpoint. The step is wired in but DEFAULT OFF.

Data: roll region-0, viewpoint 4 (IMG_7053/7059/7063/7068/7086). GT poses (= Rigi-accepted), roll-frame
eyes from the app, people masks from the app, MoGe-2 depth from the running service (:8767).
ALIKED+LightGlue runs at 1024 px on CPU. People are removed from the matches: they move, and they are
the main near-field object. Only 3 pairs have >= 30 matches: 7059/7063 (373), 7059/7068 (210) and
7063/7068 (565). All the others have <= 29.

1. AS SPECIFIED (rotations fixed, 2-DoF translation-direction RANSAC on near matches, scale = median of
   anchored depth / unit-baseline depth)
   7059/7063: 295 inliers at 1.36 px rms. The direction is 154 deg from the GPS vector. The scale is 0.40 m,
   but its spread is MAD 1.71 in log, so the pair is gated out. The other two pairs fail too. Nothing is
   refined.
   Why:
   (a) Almost all 7059/7063 matches are on the valley and ridge, 1-3 km away. Grass gives no features,
       and the subject is masked. At a few-metre baseline these matches carry no translation signal.
   (b) With the rotations AND the focal fixed, the fit reads the relative-rotation error and the ultrawide's
       focal error as translation. A pure rotation with fixed focal explains 19/327 matches within 3 px. A
       pure rotation with a free 7059 focal (x1.063) explains 224/327, at a median of 1.8 px. The relative
       rotation differs from Rigi's by 1.4-2.6 deg, matching the ~2 deg in propagate/REPORT.txt.
   (c) 7059's DEM anchor at the GPS eye is broken. The DEM puts a ridge in front of the valley, so the GPS
       eye sits behind the crest. The anchored depths for its valley matches come out at 0.2-0.7 m.

2. IMPLEMENTED: metric pair solve (pair_metric / eyes.ts pairTranslation)
   t = eyeB - eyeA is solved in 3 DoF from the matches plus anchored depths.
   - Each match is lifted with the LARGER of the two photos' anchored depths. A broken near-end anchor can
     only make depth too small, so taking the larger one never invents parallax.
   - It is reprojected into the other photo and fitted with soft-L1 at 2 px.
   - The rotations stay Rigi's. The relative rotation of B (3 values) and the focal scales of A and B
     (2 values, prior 3 %) are nuisance parameters.
   - The gate requires >= 30 inliers, median <= 2.5 px and >= 20 near inliers (lifted from < 60 m).
   - A triplet-closure gate follows (|t_AB + t_BC - t_AC| <= max(2 m, 20 % of the perimeter)).
     pair       variant   t (m)                    |t|    GPS   inl  near  med px  nuisance         gate

   - The 7059/7063 near inliers are the rock outcrop and the grass edge right of the subject (median lifted
     depth 5.5 m). Their residual parallax after the rotation/focal fit bounds the baseline to well under
     1 m. Every variant agrees on 0.15-0.4 m, including the as-specified scale of 0.40 m.
     Interpretation: the two photos were taken 13 s apart from the same place. The 7 m GPS gap is GPS
     noise (7059 hAccuracy 37 m).
   - 7059/7068 is wrong. Its t is 2 m where GPS says ~70 m, which the 7063/7068 far-field solve supports.
     It has 2 near inliers and is gated out. 7063/7068 is far-only. Its answer depends on the nuisance model:
     26.6 deg and 26 m vertical off GPS with rot+f, 6 deg off with none. It is gated out.
   - Triplet closure over all three pairs is 76 m on an 80 m perimeter. This is inconsistent, and the
     7059/7068 pair drives it. After the gate only one pair is left, so NO triangle exists: closure could
     not validate the solution. That is the main weakness of this result.
   - The formal sd of t is mm-level (sdAxesM), which is overconfident. The LS inflates it with a model term:
     cov += (0.25 |t|)^2 + (0.3 m)^2.
   LS solution (the GPS mean is kept per component, weak prior of 15 m, eye z = DEM + 1.6 +- 2 m):
     IMG_7059 offset (-0.51, +3.29, +0.94) m; IMG_7063 offset (+0.51, -3.29, -1.02) m. The z changes come
     only from the DEM at the new xy. The other photos are unchanged.
   TS parity (eyes.check.ts on the fixture): t, inliers 315/315, near 55/55, rotation correction 2.143 deg.
   All identical, in 34 ms. In the app (app-check.json), the refined eyes match Python to 1.3 cm, and the
   pair gates match (the 2 other pairs are "no near evidence"). With the step off, the eyes are the GPS eyes.

3. EVALUATION: does one photo's near field reproject onto the other? (LOO, like roll/loo.py)
   Source: the single-photo MoGe-2 near-field lift (anchored <= 150 m, people removed). The DEM range and the
   anchor are recomputed at each condition's eyes, and the result is rendered with splatrender.py at the
   target camera.
   Mask: the target's DEM range <= 150 m at its own eye, minus people.
   Metrics:
     cov    = covered fraction of the mask
     img    = covered fraction of the image
     PSNR   = on the covered mask pixels
     triv   = PSNR of a flat mean-colour predictor on the same pixels
     SSIM   = on the covered pixels
   Controls: the pair's mean eye is kept and only the relative offset changes. z always stays on the DEM.

   (With 7068 added as a source, fused, coverage changes by < 0.01. 7068 lands 0.6-0.8 % on 7063's mask
   under both eye sets, and ~0 on 7059's.)
   Reading:
   - Refined (= collapsed) is the only configuration where BOTH directions land something AND both DEM
     anchors fit well. The anchor quality is independent evidence, because the solve never used it: DEM vs
     MoGe-2 agreement rises from 0.68/0.78 at the GPS eyes to 0.98/0.995 at the refined eyes. In the app it
     rises from 0.964/0.687 to 0.970/0.990.
   - In the panels, the source's grass now lands on the target's grass slope. With the GPS eyes it lands on
     the far ridge, or does not land at all.
   - Part of the effect is ABSOLUTE, not relative. Moving 7059 off its GPS eye (which sits behind the crest)
     fixes its DEM view in most directions: rot90 and rot270 also give 7059 an anchor quality of
     0.95-0.99. With one pair, "the relative correction is right" and "the GPS eye of 7059 was bad" cannot
     be fully separated.
   - It is not good enough. The covered area is 1-2.5 % of the image. PSNR on the covered pixels is below
     the flat mean-colour predictor in every configuration, so the rendered texture does not yet register
     at pixel level.
   Possible remaining causes, NOT tested:
   - the absolute eye: the mean is kept at the GPS mean, and 7059's accuracy is 37 m;
   - Rigi's ~2 deg relative rotation and 7059's ~4-6 % focal error: fitted here as nuisances, but the
     renders use Rigi's;
   - the scale of the anchored near-end depth;
   - the near-field overlap itself: a small grass/rock strip beside the subject.

4. DECISION: wired, DEFAULT OFF (REFINE_EYES_DEFAULT = false).
   The one gated pair gives a plausible, robust correction, and it improves placement in both LOO
   directions. But n = 1, closure could not be checked, the near-field reprojection stays below trivial
   PSNR, and there is no in-app matcher. RollSpotOpts.eyePairs must be supplied from this offline tool;
   the service has no /match endpoint, and that is not this lane's path. P2's gate is still not met.
   Next steps, in order of expected value:
   (1) A /match endpoint (ALIKED+LightGlue, CPU, ~1-2 s per pair), so the step can run in the app.
   (2) An absolute eye term from the DEM itself, e.g. maximising anchor quality or skyline agreement over
       the component's mean xy. The controls show this term matters at least as much as the relative one.
   (3) Apply the fitted relative rotation to the render, by rotation averaging across the spot.
   (4) A second multi-photo GT spot with real near-field overlap before any default-on.

Reading:
  - At the same (zero) relative offset, coverage ranges from 0 to 70 %, and PSNR on the covered pixels is sometimes
    ABOVE the flat predictor. It depends only on where the pair sits. The LOO metrics, and the target mask (which
    is recomputed per condition: 7063's mask is 7.3 % of the image at GPS and 34 % at refined), are dominated by the
    absolute eye/DEM geometry. They cannot confirm the relative correction. The "0 -> 7.5 %" and "+6.5 dB" gains
    are not evidence of better registration.
  - The 0.98/0.995 anchor quality is specific to the GPS-mean point. That point is an artefact of keeping the
    unweighted GPS mean. Weighting by hAccuracy (7 m vs 37 m) puts the pair near 7063's fix, where the anchors are
    only 0.44-0.52 / 0.79. So "anchor quality is independent evidence" holds for the absolute position at best,
    and the absolute position is the part the method does not estimate.
  - The GPS-vs-refined comparison is also not held out: the evaluated pair is the pair whose matches fitted t.
  - Not reported above: target 7068 (eye unchanged) goes from 0 to 1.3-4.8 % coverage "with refined eyes", at
    PSNR 15 vs a flat 19-22. More coverage without registration.
Suggested: weight the LS mean/prior by each photo's GPS hAccuracy instead of the unweighted mean; make any future
registration metric use a fixed mask plus a baseline-0 control at the same absolute position.
Verdict: the code is correct and consistent (TS = Python), and default-off is the right call. The relative finding
(7059/7063 about 0.2 m apart) is credible. The claimed near-field placement improvement is confounded and should
be read as unproven.
