STEP INSIDE P2 EXPLORATION: POSE PROPAGATION WITHIN A VIEWPOINT                          2026-09-28
=====================================================================================================
Question: can one photo with an accepted pose (anchor A) give a usable pose for a neighbour B taken at
the same viewpoint, via a relative rotation?  R_B = relR . R_A  (R = world(ENU) -> OpenCV camera,
[right; -up; forward], tools/research/tm/cache/FORMAT.md; relR maps A-camera to B-camera coordinates).

Short answer: YES as a SEED/SUGGESTION when the two photos overlap, using classical matching
(ALIKED+LightGlue, CPU) and a pure-rotation RANSAC. DA3 /multiview is NOT usable for this: it is less
accurate, and it has no confidence signal. Evidence is thin: n = 6 real overlapping ordered pairs,
from 1 viewpoint and 3 photos, all "approx" GT. Everything else is synthetic.

Files
  run_propagate.py   estimators + data (real viewpoint pairs, synthetic pure-rotation pairs, different-place negatives)
  analyze.py         raw.json -> results.json (distributions, gate sweep, chosen gate per set)
  results.json       all numbers + per-pair rows;  raw.json = full estimator outputs;  cache/ = per-pair cache
  propagate.check.ts TS parity with Python (npx tsx tools/nearfield/propagate/propagate.check.ts -> OK)
  wild_dev_case.json one unverified dev-split suggestion (wc_0054 -> wc_0086), see "Recall"
  src/lib/nearfield/propagate.ts  proposePose(anchorPose, relR, targetVfov, aspects, evidence) + PROPAGATE_GATE

Data (dev only; data_v3 and the test split never touched)
  real   GT viewpoint on Niederhorn: IMG_7053, 7059, 7063, 7068, 7086 (7-208 m apart). All 20 ordered
         pairs. Only 7059/7063/7068 actually overlap (6 ordered pairs; GT overlap 0.30-0.93). The other
         14 pairs point 96-159 deg apart, so they are natural negatives. The anchor uses GT
         yaw/pitch/roll/f. The target uses only its EXIF vfov (photos.json), as the app would.
         GT quality: 7053/7086 'good', 7059/7063/7068 'approx' (notes: ~0.3-1 deg each; 7059 rms 18 px).
  synth  120 pairs. Two virtual cameras are warped out of ONE real photo by a known pure rotation
         (source photos: GT-12 photos except the viewpoint, and wild DEV photos with a focal). Each
         pair has a random yaw offset of 0-1.4x hfov, pitch up to 6 deg, roll up to 8 deg, a zoom
         ratio of 0.8-1.25, and a photometric/JPEG/blur change on B. relR is exact.
         111 pairs overlap, 9 don't. There is no parallax and no time change, so this is an UPPER BOUND.
  neg    60 pairs of different wild DEV photos (different places). Any propagation is wrong.

Estimators
  rot   ALIKED (2048 kp) + LightGlue on CPU, 2-point RANSAC on bearing vectors (4 px), Kabsch refinement.
  ess   Same matches, cv2.findEssentialMat + recoverPose.
  da3   POST /multiview (DA3-Base, processRes 504) on [A, B]; relR = c2w[1][:3,:3]^T. The median time
        per pair was 0.31 s warm on MPS, plus the model load.

Why real errors are ~2 deg when synthetic ones are ~0.02 deg (not resolved; n = 6):
  - Focal is not the cause. Swapping in GT intrinsics for EXIF, or self-calibrating the target focal
    (scale 0.85-1.15), leaves the errors at 1.4-3.6 deg.
  - The rot estimates agree with each other. Cycle closure over 7059/7063/7068 is 0.63 / 0.99 deg,
    and forward/backward disagreement is 0.02-0.61 deg. So the estimates are self-consistent and
    differ from GT by a nearly systematic ~2 deg.
  - Two explanations remain. One is the 'approx' GT: two poses each ~0.5-1 deg off, and 7059's pin is
    ambiguous. The other is parallax from near foreground, since the cameras are 7-74 m apart. The
    7063<->7068 pair (73 m apart) has the smallest error (1.5-1.8), which argues against parallax
    dominating. A DEM render check at the propagated pose would separate the two. It was not done here.

Confidence gate (src/lib/nearfield/propagate.ts PROPAGATE_GATE; results.json gateChosen/gateSweepAll)
  PASS if method = rot, inliers >= 40, inlier RMS <= 3.5 px (1024 px), predicted overlap >= 0.1, and,
  when supplied: fwd/bwd <= 1.5 deg, triplet cycle <= 1.5 deg, |pitch - gravity| <= 3 and
  |roll - gravity| <= 3 deg, baseline <= 250 m.
  Chosen gate, by set:
    real overlapping 6/6 pass, all within 3.7 deg | real non-overlapping 0/14 | synth 104/111, all
    <= 0.13 deg (the misses have 8-30 inliers at overlap <= 0.08) | synth non-overlapping 0/9 |
    negatives 0/60. The TS check reproduces 6/6 and 0/14 on the real pairs.
  Separation is wide. The best wrong pair had 11 inliers; any threshold from 15 to 100 gives zero
  false passes on all 200 pairs (the sweep's only false pass is at 10 inliers with no fwd/bwd check).
  Inliers are the signal that works. Forward/backward consistency is weak: with the same matcher, B->A
  is almost the exact inverse of A->B even when both are wrong (0.0 deg on the wrong synthetic pairs).
  Gravity agrees on real pairs within 2.2 deg pitch and 1.8 deg roll (7068's own GT is 2.2 deg off its
  gravity pitch).
  DA3 has no usable confidence. The wire carries none. Its error correlates with its disagreement
  with rot (Spearman 0.998 on synthetic pairs), but only because rot is nearly exact there, so "agrees
  with rot" means "use rot". When rot fails (no overlap), DA3 is also wrong (median 43 deg on real
  non-overlapping pairs). DA3 adds no recall and is excluded by the gate (methods: ["rot"]).

Recall (what this could buy; nothing here is claimed as a verified gain)
  - GT-12: the only overlapping cluster is 7059/7063/7068. With any one of them accepted, the other two
    get a suggestion within ~2-4 deg of GT, a useful seed for the DEM cascade/matcher (7063 is
    currently a CPU-cascade reject after the FF35 change; memory: project-accuracy-state).
  - Wild dev: wc_0054 (blind-verified correct) and wc_0086 (no correct pose from any method; all
    three v2 candidates were verified wrong) are 58 m apart and do overlap (I looked at both photos:
    same lake arm, different season). rot: 80 inliers, 2.19 px RMS, fwd/bwd 0.06 deg, predicted
    overlap 0.41, so it PASSES the gate. The suggestion for wc_0086 is yaw 71.2, pitch -15.7, roll
    -2.8 (EXIF vfov 39.6). It is UNVERIFIED. I did not render or judge an overlay: self-checking is
    the known trap, and it needs a blind verifier. If it verifies, it is one wild recall item that
    every current method misses.
  - Co-location is rare in the Commons benchmark: 1 dev pair within 1 km. The win is for camera rolls
    (the user's own photos, where viewpoints of 3-10 photos are common; roll.ts groups them within
    250 m), not for single uploads.

Limits / honest caveats
  - Real evidence is 6 ordered pairs (3 unordered) from one place and one afternoon. Synthetic pairs
    have no parallax, no lens distortion and no scene change, so they only show the machinery and the
    negative gate.
  - The anchor's own error (accepted poses ~0.1-0.5 deg) adds directly to the target's error.
  - Parallax: untested beyond 74 m between overlapping cameras, and with near foreground it can
    exceed the error seen here. The gate caps the baseline at 250 m, but that cap is a guess.
  - Moving content (clouds, people) was not stress-tested. The negatives were static landscapes.
  - SUGGESTION ONLY. proposePose returns kind "suggestion" and never an accept. Using it for an accept
    needs the prereg in PREREG_DRAFT.txt and blind verification under the frozen product rule.

DEM RENDER CHECK: GT ERROR vs PARALLAX (added 2026-09-29; render_check/)
=====================================================================================================
Method frozen before running: render_check/METHOD.txt (sha1 in METHOD.sha1). Deviations: DEVIATIONS.txt.
Code: render_check/skyline.ts (the app's sky model -> photo skyline), check.py (frozen analysis -> out/result.json),
posthoc.py (POST HOC -> out/posthoc.json). Overlays: out/overlay_<id>.jpg (white GT, orange/red propagated,
green DEM fit, cyan photo skyline). Everything is CPU (dem.py horizon renderer, Mapterhorn). No browser or GPU render.
  How it works: the DEM skyline is rendered at the GT pose, at each propagated pose and at a free DEM-skyline fit F
  (yaw/pitch/roll, GT focal). Each is scored against the photo's own skyline as a truncated mean |row delta|
  (12 px cap) on 1024-px images, over columns whose DEM skyline is at least 1 km away. A separate parallax
  simulation ray-casts each rot inlier from anchor A into the DEM. It then re-projects the hit from B's GPS eye
  and fits the pure rotation that DEM-scale parallax alone would produce.
  Deviation D1: the GT eyes (GPS altitude) are 5-18 m BELOW Mapterhorn ground at all three GPS points. The GT
  was built on AWS Terrarium, which puts the ground at 1859-1903 m. Every eye is therefore ground + 1.6 m (the
  GT's own 'max' rule, applied to Mapterhorn). So the absolute skyline fit of the GT poses is also degraded here.

Frozen result: INCONCLUSIVE (decision rule 0: fit precision).
  IMG_7059 (13 mm ultrawide) cannot be fitted to the skyline. The bootstrap geodesic RMS of F is 5.9 deg (the
  limit is 0.5), and F lands 10 deg from GT. The far skyline in 7059 is short and flat, and from a 1.6 m DEM eye
  at the cliff-edge lookout the near plateau hides most of it. The frozen parallax simulation is also invalid for
  anchor 7059: all 82 and 92 inlier rays hit the DEM within ~4 m (the same eye/lookout artefact). Its median
  bias_par of 64 deg is therefore meaningless.
  7063 and 7068 fit well: bootstrap RMS 0.10 and 0.12 deg; an eye 15 m higher moves F by 0.43 and 0.30 deg.

POST HOC (after seeing the frozen result; exploratory, n = 1 viewpoint)
  P2: the parallax simulation with DEM hits < 500 m dropped (0 hits left from anchor 7059).
  P4: implied pose R_B = Cpar^T . relR_rot . R_F_A. This undoes the simulated parallax rotation Cpar and uses
  the DEM-fitted anchor F instead of GT.

  residual from 5.43 to 1.41 px.

Conclusion
  Frozen rule: INCONCLUSIVE, because 7059 cannot be fitted. Post hoc, the ~2 deg error has two separable parts:
  - PARALLAX is real, and it is the terrain itself, not near foreground. At a 73 m baseline, the matched terrain
    at 2.5-3 km gives a simulated pure-rotation bias of 1.2-1.7 deg, and the 7063<->7068 error matches it in
    direction. REPORT's earlier argument that this pair's small error 'argues against parallax dominating' is
    wrong: its error is mostly parallax, which partly cancels 7063's GT roll error.
  - GT ERROR is also real. 7063's 'approx' GT roll looks ~1.5 deg off (7068's GT agrees with the DEM fit). For
    7059, the 7059<->7063 pair was shot 13 s apart (baseline ~0; simulated parallax 0.12 deg), so parallax
    cannot explain its 2.2-2.3 deg. The implied 7059 poses from the 7063 and 7068 anchors, parallax-corrected
    and F-anchored, agree with each other within ~0.9 deg and sit ~2 deg of yaw below 7059's GT (implied yaw
    2.1-2.5 vs GT 4.39 and the GT note's 3.3-4.4 range). But the skyline cannot confirm this: the implied poses
    score worse than GT (8.5-9.5 vs 7.07 px) on a skyline that is ill-conditioned anyway. So for 7059 it stays
    open between GT yaw error and an ultrawide lens-model bias in rot. It is not parallax.
  Correcting for parallax works. The DEM-depth parallax correction plus a DEM-fitted anchor brings the 73 m pair
  to 0.36-0.41 deg of the target's own DEM fit. Uncorrected, the skyline residual at the propagated pose is 7.2
  and 5.5 px; corrected, it is 2.9 and 3.6 px.

Implications for PREREG_DRAFT.txt (notes only; the draft is unchanged)
  - The 250 m baseline cap is too loose for a pure-rotation seed. The measured bias is ~baseline / (matched-
    terrain distance): about 1.4 deg at 73 m and ~3 km, so up to ~5 deg at 250 m, which reaches the 5 deg seed
    radius (H2 p90 <= 5). Either (a) cap the baseline much lower (~50 m for <= 1 deg at >= 3 km), or (b) freeze a
    parallax-corrected composition: DEM depth for the anchor's inliers, known target eye, then a rotation-only
    solve, as check.py/posthoc.py do. Choose before freezing; do not tune it on the held-out set.
  - Dev 'approx' GT is not accurate enough to score sub-2-deg propagation error (7063 roll ~1.5 deg; 7059 yaw
    unresolved). The prereg already scores by blind verification, not GT, which is the right call.
  - The GT eyes of the Niederhorn photos are below Mapterhorn ground (Terrarium-era eyes). Worth a GT-maintenance
    note: this was not changed here.
  - Anchor-at-a-lookout trap: a ground + 1.6 m DEM eye at a cliff edge can hide the terrain the matches sit on
    (7059). Any DEM-depth correction must detect rays that hit within a few metres and fall back or skip.
  Caveats: n = 1 viewpoint, 2 usable targets, 4 usable ordered pairs. The analysis is post hoc. F and the
  parallax simulation depend on GPS positions (hAccuracy 7-37 m) and a guessed eye height.
