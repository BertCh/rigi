P1 EXIT GATE: "the split removes >= 80% of person/hut/tree drape smear". Measured 2026-09-28.
VERDICT: NOT MET, on either renderer.

Setup
- Labels: labels.json, 99 hand polygons/boxes on the 14 posed GT photos (quality good/approx). Drawn blind
  from gridded downscaled photos BEFORE any nearfield code was read or run (file mtime precedes all grid-*).
  unsure=true marks objects that may be >300 m away or already in the DEM, or outlines that are rough.
  Classes: person, building, tree, pole, structure (fences/boards/walls), vehicle (boat), animal, rock.
- Metric (scripts/nearfield/smear-measure.mjs): taken from the drape shader's own rule (materials.ts and
  deck terrain-layer.ts): photo pixel puv lands on terrain iff seen(puv) > minProjectRange (80 m) and
  mask(puv) <= 0.5. seen is engine.sampleAt range, the readback of the same range buffer. The mask is the
  texture the world drape binds: OFF = the people mask (protectPeople); ON = three nf.masks.worldFg, or deck
  drapeMask().photoFg. Sampling is bilinear like the GPU, on a 320x240 grid of photo cells.
  Smear = a labelled cell that is draped. It is photo-space and independent of the viewpoint; an offset
  viewer sees a subset of it. I did not render an offset view.
- "prod" = the product: a photo under ANCHOR_MIN_QUALITY shows no scene, so ON == OFF. "forced" = the
  scene's mask applied anyway.
- Code fingerprint 572f71d7a5b8 (fingerprint.sh), stable across the final three and deck runs (run-log.txt).
  Earlier runs made while other sessions were editing scene.ts/ground.ts/near-dem.ts gave different
  numbers. One transient deck state marked the whole 4-11 km mountainside Object on IMG_6958, with 83%
  collateral. Treat this as a moving target.

Why it fails (why.mjs: split class of the labelled cells that still smear, forced)
1. Most leftover smear is split class Far: the anchored model range is over nearRadius=150 m, or there is
   no model depth. Examples: three tree 14419 Far vs 7811 Terrain; building 2821 Far vs 229 Terrain. Huts
   and trees at 100-300 m (IMG_7130 chalets, IMG_7131 house, IMG_7018/7033 trees against the lake) are
   never Object. Raising nearRadius is not enough: at 300 m non-person removal only reaches 47% (three),
   while collateral grows. Trees also split as Terrain (about 8k cells): their model depth is too close to
   the DEM range behind them.
2. The quality gate hides the scene on 4/14 photos in three and 2/14 in deck. Anchor quality for the same
   photo and pose is unstable between renderers and runs: IMG_7053 three 0.03 vs deck 0.57; 7059 0.95 vs
   0.00; 7068 0.00 vs 0.67; 7155 0.00 vs 0.19; 7086 three 0.10 then 0.28. The 72% (three) pole and 70%
   structure removal comes almost entirely from gated photos (7053, 7155), so the product gets none of it.
3. Classic drape already hides most near-field smear. minProjectRange=80 m cuts every cell whose terrain
   behind is within 80 m (three: 127k of 210k person cells, all boat and rock cells). What is left are
   objects seen against terrain more than 80 m away, which are exactly the far huts and trees in point 1.
4. three and deck classic drapes differ. The deck range buffer drops terrain near the eye (IMG_6958
   bottom row: 6.8 m in three vs 31.7 m in deck; near-dem.ts comment "query buffer discards terrain within
   nearDiscard / 2"). Near fence posts in deck therefore drape onto the far valley: IMG_7068 posts give
   3908 smear cells in deck vs 229 in three. Deck vehicle smear (IMG_6971 boat, 1145 cells) exists only
   because of this, and the split removes 99% of it.
Where it works: poles and fence posts on accepted photos (5495 97%, 7053 98% forced), IMG_6971
people/boat (94%/99%), the IMG_7086 hut (three 45%, deck 28%).

Caveats: labels are coarse (about 1-2% of width), and the label edge band is reported separately. The
person-class residual (about 1.4% of person cells) is mostly label or people-mask edge. The diagnostic
nearRadius variants rebuild the scene in-page (scene.ts buildNearFieldScene, DEM grid via sampleAt rather
than nearFieldDemRange); they are not the product path. Cells are photo pixels, not ground area.
