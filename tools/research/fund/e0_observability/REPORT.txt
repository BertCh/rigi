1. Design notes (from PROTOCOL; why it differs from "at the ref pose")
 - Labels from F1 stage: SUCCESS 19 (T6 correct HIGH), ROT = search/matching/refine 16, POS = position 9,
   OTHER = decision/verification 6.
 - None of the 9 POS photos has a correct ref (6/9 have no manifest heading). All 19 successes do. A ref-pose analysis
   therefore has POS n = 0, and mixing ref yaw for successes with anything else for failures leaks the label.
   Primary analysis A is uniform and label-free: at the stated eye, from the 24 ring views, averaged over 72 azimuths
   (w* = median over azimuths; I_eye = mean over hfov0-wide windows, pitch-0 band clipped to the ring's coverage).
   Secondary: B = at the manifest heading (label-free, n 15/11/4/3), C = at the correct ref (xyz of the ref view;
   ROT n = 5, POS n = 0), D = "best available" (ref, then heading, then A). D is label-leaky and is shown only to
   demonstrate that leak.
 - One implementation fix was made before any AUROC was computed. Skyline bins were first invalidated whenever any
   ring view clipped them. They are now judged only by the owning (nearest-centre) ring view, whose top edge is highest
   at that azimuth.

3. What the numbers mean (honest reading)
 a) The rotation predictor is confounded by a cache gap. POST HOC: every photo with ≥ 50% of its 360° skyline clipped
    gets w* = 360 ("never unique"): 13/13 photos, against 1/37 of the rest. The clipping comes from skyline above the
    top edge of the pitch-0 ring (ring vfov 17–57°). Spearman(hfov/w*, clipped fraction) = −0.57. Among photos with
    < 30% clipping, AUROC = 0.54 (6 vs 9). So FOV/w* has not been tested properly. The failure groups do have
    wider median w* (ROT 18.5° vs 10°), and ROT vs rest gives 0.664 (p = 0.03, secondary). Both are too entangled with
    clipping to claim. Fixing this needs a pitched ring (e.g. +20°/+40° at the stated eye). That means rendering, which
    the brief says to ask about first, so it was NOT done.
 b) Eye information. The pre-registered direction ("low kept information → position failure") is null (0.50).
    Reverse direction: position failures have MORE total parallax information at the stated eye (A 0.708, p = 0.04;
    B 0.98 with only n = 4). This fits a stated-eye error mattering exactly where parallax is large: a big foreground
    in frame, the F1 "44% within 300 m" observation. It does not fit "the eye is unobservable". Caveats:
    - it was not the primary direction;
    - 5/9 POS are gross (> 400 m) position errors, so geometry "at the stated eye" is not the photo's geometry;
    - I_tot is dominated by ground a few metres from the eye (see c).
 c) Where the information lives (item 5). Under the Σ(1/d)² metric, ≥ 99.9% of eye information lies below 250 m for
    nearly every photo, failing or succeeding. A median f250: SUCC 0.9999, ROT 0.9998, POS 1.0000, OTHER 0.9999.
    Ref-view C: SUCC 0.97, ROT 0.59 (n = 5). Inside the kept band (≥ 250 m), 94–99% lies in 250 m–2 km in every group.
    The 250 m cut therefore discards nearly all raw eye information for successes as well. It cannot by itself separate
    failures from successes.
 d) The Cramér–Rao bound is not the binding limit. Assumptions: 500 matches uniform over ≥ 250 m terrain, 1 px noise,
    rotation marginalised, focal known. Horizontal eye 1σ, analysis A median: SUCC 0.44 m, ROT 0.26 m, POS 0.48 m.
    The worst photo is 3.9 m (wc_0020). The ref view gives 0.1–5 m. Every photo, including the failures, carries
    enough mid-field parallax in principle to fix the eye far better than GPS (5–15 m). The practical limit is model
    and measurement error: near/mid-field render fidelity (z14 blur, smeared faces, DSM objects), appearance gap,
    outliers, DEM error. It is not geometric information content. The mid-field bound is optimistic by construction
    because it assumes correct correspondences there.

4. Implications for the plan (interpretation, not tested here)
 - E0's premise "solvability is predictable from geometry alone" fails its fixed test. Geometry-only predictors do
   not separate the F1 buckets at n ≈ 50.
 - Lever question (E3 vs FOV): no support for "low I_eye → position failure". There is weak, direction-reversed
   support for "lots of near-field parallax + wrong stated eye → failure". Together with (d), this points E3 at
   FIDELITY of the 250 m–2 km band and at an eye search/veto, not at "more information". E3 is not contradicted.
 - FOV/w* (multi-photo/pano) is untested because of the ring clipping. Re-run (a) if a pitched ring is rendered
   (≈ 2 extra views × 24 yaws × 50 photos, stated eye only).

5. Limitations
 - n = 19/16/9/6. CIs span ~0.4 wide. F1 labels are unblinded post-hoc judgements (±2–3 per bucket).
 - The ring covers only the pitch-0 band. The near field below −9…−28° and the skyline above +9…+28° are missing
   in A/B.
 - Sanity check: Spearman of I_no250 between ring (at ref yaw) and ref view = 0.76 (n = 30). The ring's within-band
   f250 is lower than the ref view's (median 0.978 vs 0.990).
 - The skyline elevation is quantised at the ring's stride-2 sample (~0.08°), so the 0.05–0.1° floors are close to
   that quantisation.
 - Roll and focal are ignored in w*. Skylines are the rendered DEM's, not the photo's: clouds and occluders are
   ignored.
