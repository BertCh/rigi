# Matching v2: research sweep (September 2026)

Scope: what to change in Stage 1 (yaw search) and Stage 2 (ALIKED+LightGlue photo↔render → lift → rotation+focal solve) given the failure classes in `reports/test-results.md` / `reports/matcher*.md`. This complements `reports/Mountain photo georeferencing SoTA.md` and does not repeat it (LandscapeAR, Baboud/Baatz, RoMa v2 as a concept, MASt3R licence, iPhone EXIF priors, skyline extraction are all covered there).

Conventions: **[V]** = verified today against a primary source (repo, LICENSE file, HF API, arXiv). **[U]** = unverified or my estimate. Runtimes for "our laptop" are estimates unless stated as measured.

---

## Executive summary

1. **LoMa (ECCV 2026 Oral) is the drop-in upgrade for ALIKED+LightGlue.** It is a LightGlue-style matcher over DaD keypoints and DeDoDe descriptors with a frozen **DINOv2 ViT-L/14** trunk. Code is MIT, the matcher part inherits LightGlue's Apache-2.0, and DINOv2 is Apache-2.0, so there is no DINOv3 or NC encumbrance. Against ALIKED+LightGlue it scores **+29.5 mAA on WxBS and +18.6 mAA on HardMatch**. HardMatch includes aerial-vs-ground pairs, pairs taken a century apart, sketch↔photo and night–day pairs, which is the closest public proxy we have for photo↔satellite-draped render. The cost is a ViT-L forward pass per image, so expect a few seconds per image on the M3 Pro CPU [U]. Try this first.
2. **Cross-modal-trained matchers (MatchAnything, MINIMA, XoFTR) are a secondary tool, not a replacement.** They help where the gap is really one of modality (hillshade, snow versus summer ortho, haze). There is no public benchmark of any of them on photo↔DEM render. A 2026 SAR↔optical study ranked RoMa and XoFTR (3.0 px) just ahead of MatchAnything-ELoFTR and MINIMA-RoMa (3.4 px). **Licence trap:** upstream MatchAnything moved to a "Project Registration License" in 2026, while the HF transformers port card still says Apache-2.0.
3. **GeoCalib should become the prior for roll, pitch and focal.** It is Apache-2.0 code with CC-BY-4.0 weights (116 MB), accepts focal or gravity priors, and gets roll within 1° on 83–86% of outdoor images. Pitch is weaker (1° AUC 32–55%) and FoV weaker still (10° AUC 48–60%). Use it to re-centre the pitch/roll fan (the narrow-FOV failure class) and as a wide focal prior (the no-EXIF class). AnyCalib (Apache-2.0) estimates FoV better (vFoV AUC@10° 65 vs 60 on LaMAR) but outputs no gravity.
4. **The 9-to-40-view LightGlue sweep can be replaced by one dense-feature circular correlation.** Take per-column pooled DINOv2 (or LoMa-descriptor) features of the photo and of the cached 360° render ring, and correlate them over yaw, in the same way we already correlate the horizon profile. Published support is indirect: LAYS column-aggregation yaw voting reaches sub-degree yaw on 35–53% of limited-FOV ground↔BEV images (no code), and MCLoc shows that frozen pretrained dense features work for render-and-compare. **There is no mountain-specific evidence, so this must be benchmarked on our dev split.**
5. **Mountain-specific literature 2023–2026 is thin.** Nothing new beats LandscapeAR on GeoPose3K. The only relevant new numbers are historical-photo horizon orientation (204 Alpine images: 63% at manual-orientation accuracy and 22% more usable as initialisation) and Geo-LoFTR (Mars; synthetic-to-real transfer "on par" with baselines, CC BY-NC-SA). LTM (Jul 2026) needs known poses and has no code.
6. **No published work matches mono-depth discontinuities to DEM depth layers for pose.** Use MoGe-2 (MIT code and weights) or DA3-Small/Base (Apache-2.0) only as a near-field occluder mask and ordinal check. Depth Pro weights are Apple research-only, and DA3-Large/Giant are CC BY-NC.
7. **Eye position: refine only when near-field inliers exist.** The evidence says distant skylines cannot fix position (LandscapeAR: no gain within about 200 m), and dense render-and-compare converges only for small offsets (MCLoc: robust up to about 5 m, degrading at 10 m). A depth-stratified 6-DoF+f solve (PoseLib) using lifted matches under about 2 km, with a strong position prior, is the practical route.

**MPS determinism.** No wrapped model documents it [V: vismatch says MPS is untested]. Our own finding is that LightGlue on MPS gives wrong match counts under contention (`reports/matcher-service.md`). Assume every attention-based matcher needs a CPU path. Backbone-only passes (DINOv2) on MPS are probably fine to cache but must be checked by repeat-run hashing [U].

**Photo vs render evidence, overall.** There is still no benchmark of any 2025–26 matcher on photo↔textured-DEM render. The SAR study notes that DINOv2-backbone RoMa is cross-modal-robust with no cross-modal training, and that "protocol choices change error by up to 33×", more than the gap between matchers.

## Q6. Eye position off by 100–500 m

- **Observability.** A distant skyline does not constrain position; LandscapeAR could not improve fixes already within about 200 m (prior doc). Near-field lifted matches (depth < ~2 km) do constrain it.
- **Recipe [U, engineering].**
  1. After the rotation+f solve, split the lifted inliers by render depth.
  2. If there are at least 30 near-field inliers spread over at least 2 image quadrants, run a **6-DoF+f** solve: PoseLib `estimate_absolute_pose` with an unknown-focal minimal solver inside LO-RANSAC. Add a Gaussian position prior (σ ≈ 150 m horizontal, with height snapped to the DEM + 1.7 m).
  3. Re-render at the new eye and re-match once; accept only if the skyline residual and inliers both improve.
  4. Otherwise run a small eye grid (e.g. 3×3 at 150 m) scored by the **fast skyline** cue only. `basinGrid` already exists, so extend it to the eye position.
- **Evidence on limits.** Dense render-and-compare converges only for small offsets (MCLoc ≤5 m urban; Render2Loc ±5 m). For 100–500 m, rely on geometric PnP from near-field matches, not on photometric refinement.
- PoseLib: BSD-3, `pip install poselib` ([repo](https://github.com/PoseLib/PoseLib)) [V: known].
